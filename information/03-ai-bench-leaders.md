# AI-Bench: vision for leaders (participant deck)

**Draft 0.1 · 16 October 2026.** The vision behind the marketing deck for people who would **join and pay**: owners, directors and senior operations leads of Plymouth-area organisations. Built from the October 2026 AI-Bench concept and the decisions in [02](02-ai-b-partners.md). Labels: **[P]** planned; **[A]** assumption to test; **[secondary]** cited from secondary sources.

---

## 1. The promise

> **Make one real AI change in your organisation every 90 days, tested, measured and kept or dropped, alongside a small group of local leaders doing the same.**

## 2. What leaders are facing

- Pressure to "do something with AI", and a lot of hype and vendor noise.
- Staff already using AI tools, with no shared rules on what is safe.
- Trials that impress in a demo but do not change how work gets done.
- Nobody to compare notes with: nearly half of UK small-business owners say they have no one to discuss business issues with (American Express, March 2026, n=500) [secondary].
- Real risk when AI is used without structure: unrestricted AI help raised practice scores but cut later exam scores by 17% in a 2025 study; structured use avoided the harm ([Bastani et al., PNAS 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nas/journl/v122y2025pe2422633122.html)).

## 3. What AI-Bench is

A **peer group of 8–12 leaders** from non-competing organisations, meeting **once a month for three hours in person**, with an **online clinic** between sessions [P].

**It is not:** consulting (we do not do it for you), a course (no syllabus to sit through), a networking club (no pitching), or coaching.

## 4. How it works: the 90-day cycle

| Month | You | Evidence you keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Choose and baseline | Pick one recurring workflow (proposals, invoice queries, meeting notes, scheduling, reporting); record how it works today | Time per task, error and rework rate, who does it, data sensitivity |
| 2. Change and use | Apply one bounded AI change learned at the bench; use it for real at least five times | Usage log, surprises, failures |
| 3. Measure and decide | Compare with the baseline; check your team can still do it, explain it and fix it **without** AI; keep, adapt or drop | Before and after numbers, cost, risk notes, the decision |

Over a year: **four tested workflows per member, about forty per group**, building a shared evidence library of what actually works in local organisations (anonymised, with consent).

## 5. A monthly session

| Segment | Minutes | What happens |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Arrive, coffee, quick wins | 15 | Informal |
| **The round** | 40 | Each member, four minutes: last month's experiment, the numbers, one surprise, the decision |
| **Hot seat** | 35 | One member brings a deeper problem; the group asks questions before anyone gives advice |
| **Bench time** | 60 | A hands-on technique of the month, laptops open, applied to your own workflow |
| **Commitments** | 20 | Next month's experiment and its measure |
| Close | 10 | Logistics |

**Between sessions:** a monthly online clinic (60–90 minutes); a members' channel; for Plus members, onboarding and a quarterly check-in.

## 6. The year: twelve techniques

| # | Technique | Covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Choosing and baselining a workflow; **AI risk and data basics** (what never to paste in; confidentiality; data protection) | Foundation, safety |
| 2 | Drafting and reviewing documents well | Writing |
| 3 | **Your own knowledge assistant**, grounded in your documents | Retrieval (RAG), in business form |
| 4 | Meetings: transcripts, summaries, follow-ups (with consent) | Administration |
| 5 | Spreadsheets and data analysis, with verification habits | Analysis |
| 6 | Customer-facing AI (FAQs, email triage) with guardrails and human escalation | Service |
| 7 | No-code automations with AI | Automation |
| 8 | **Delegating multi-step tasks to agents**: specify, supervise, check | Agents |
| 9 | Evaluating AI output: test sets, spot checks, error budgets | Quality |
| 10 | **People: staff adoption, learning, and keeping the skill** | The human side |
| 11 | Costs, vendors, lock-in, choosing tools | Commercial and governance |
| 12 | Annual review: the evidence library; next year's plan | Reflection |

Governance topics (for example 1 and 11) may be led with an independent governance partner [P; partner to be confirmed]. The human side runs through every month, in the spirit of PyPlym's Human × Machine approach.

## 7. Who it is for

**A good fit:** the owner, MD or senior operations lead of an organisation with roughly **3–100 staff**; already trying ChatGPT, Copilot or Claude without changing how work is done; able to give half a day a month and willing to share numbers with peers. Sectors that fit well: professional services; marine and engineering firms; care and health providers; hospitality and tourism; property and lettings; charities and social enterprises.

**Not a fit:** someone who wants the AI done for them (a separate, paid **sprint** can do that; see the consulting offer), or engineers wanting deep technical work (see **AI Workbench**).

**Group rules:** non-competing members; a mix of sectors; similar seniority.

## 10. Trust

- **What is said in the room stays in the room.** Numbers are shared within the group; anything published is anonymised and consented.
- **Nobody sells.** No pitching by members, partners or the facilitator.
- **Your data stays yours.** Bench time uses your own tools and accounts; nothing sensitive is pasted into tools without the safety basics from month 1.
