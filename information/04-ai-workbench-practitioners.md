# AI Workbench: vision for practitioners (participant deck)

**Draft 0.1 · 16 October 2026.** The vision behind the marketing deck for people who would **join and pay**: developers, analysts, data people and power users who can write some Python. Format agreed on 16 October. Labels: **[P]** planned; **[A]** assumption to test.

---

## 1. The promise

> **In six weeks, build a real AI tool and evaluate it properly, with a small cohort in Plymouth, from the human side and at real technical depth.**

## 2. Why practitioners need this

- Tutorials show demos; real systems need **evaluation, guardrails, monitoring and honest failure analysis**.
- Agents, retrieval and memory are where AI work is going, and where most of the pitfalls are.
- AI can make you faster without making you better: in one study, more confidence in AI went with less critical thinking ([Lee et al., CHI 2025](https://advait.org/files/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf)). AI Workbench builds the judgement as well as the tool.
- Learning alone is slow; a small cohort with a real project and a demo date gets things finished.

## 3. Format [P]

| Element | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| Cohort | About 10–12 people |
| Length | Six weeks |
| Sessions | One weekly evening session in Plymouth (about 2.5 hours) |
| Between sessions | Materials, discussion and code review in the cohort's space on ProDanX |
| Project | One each: your own problem, or a **live brief** (a real, consented problem, possibly from a local organisation) |
| Finish | **Demo night**: each person shows a working tool |
| Online | Possibly in future cohorts |

## 4. Six weeks, following the Human × Machine spine

| Week | Human angle | Build |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | How we **learn** | Tutors, and an evaluation baseline for your project |
| 2 | How we **delegate** | Agents and tool use |
| 3 | How we **remember** | Retrieval (RAG) and AI memory |
| 4 | How we **judge** | Evaluation and guardrails |
| 5 | How we **act** | Deployment and monitoring |
| 6 | How we **create together** | Demo night |

Each week: a short human-side framing, a technical session with real code, and project time with help.

## 5. What you leave with

- **A working AI tool**, with an evaluation harness showing how well it works and where it fails.
- **A portfolio piece**, published if you choose.
- **Methods you can reuse**: baselines, evaluation sets, guardrails, monitoring.
- **A peer network** of practitioners in Plymouth.

## 6. Who it is for

**A good fit:** comfortable writing basic Python; have used AI tools; want to build something real. Developers, analysts, data and engineering staff, technical career changers, PyPlym regulars ready for more.

**Not a fit:** complete beginners (start at **PyPlym**, where no coding is needed); business leaders wanting organisational change (see **AI-Bench**).

**Prerequisites:** a laptop; basic Python; a free account with an AI provider or a local model (guidance given).
