# AI-Bench and AI Workbench: partner vision

**Draft 0.1 · 16 October 2026.** The vision behind a partner deck for organisations that support the programmes rather than join them: **Monument AI** (knowledge partner), **Plymouth City Council** (possible seat sponsor) and **local employers such as Babcock and Helsing** (possible seat sponsors). Participant decks are documents 03 and 04. "AI-B" is internal shorthand only; externally these are **two programmes from EnReal**.

**Structure update, 17 October:** AI-Bench and AI Workbench are **EnReal programmes**. Sponsors can now be offered **one package across two audiences**: the Plymouth chapter (PyPlym) and EnReal online, with one report. Quote actual member numbers only ([00 §2.1](00-vision-foundation.md)).

## 1. In one sentence

> **Two practical programmes that help Plymouth's businesses and people make real, measured changes with AI: AI-Bench for leaders, AI Workbench for practitioners. Partners can sponsor seats for those who would otherwise miss out.**

## 2. The two programmes

| | **AI-Bench** | **AI Workbench** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| For | Owners, directors and senior leaders of local organisations (about 3–100 staff) | Developers, analysts and power users ready to build applied and agentic AI |
| Format [P] | A peer group of 8–12; one 3-hour session a month plus an online clinic; a 90-day cycle per member | A cohort of about 10–12 over 6 weeks; one weekly evening session in Plymouth (~2.5 h); weeks follow the Human × Machine spine; one project each; demo night [agreed 16 Oct] |
| What each person does | Makes **one real AI change in their own organisation**, measures it, and learns from the others' results | Builds a **working AI tool for a real problem**, properly evaluated |
| Method | "One workflow better": baseline → bounded change → use → measure time, quality, rework and risk → check people can still do it without AI → keep, adapt or drop | Build → evaluate → harden; the same honesty about failure as PyPlym |
| Led by | Tomasz as expert facilitator; knowledge-partner sessions on governance | Tomasz |
| Price [A] | Founding member £150/month (12 months); standard £195/month | About £395 individual; about £695 organisation-funded; sponsored places at the organisation price |
| Recruited through | Business networks and partners, **not** PyPlym | PyPlym and practitioner networks |
| Status | Concept researched; discovery conversations January–March 2027 | Format agreed; runs when enough people have paid |

**How they connect, optionally:** AI Workbench projects could use real, consented problems brought by AI-Bench members ("live briefs"), so leaders see a prototype and practitioners work on something real. Terms on confidentiality and ownership would be agreed first.

## 3. Why partners would support this

1. **AI adoption in small organisations is shallow.** Many report "adopting" AI, few use it extensively, and lack of skills is the barrier most often named (secondary survey evidence cited in the AI-Bench concept) [secondary].
2. **Leaders lack peers to test ideas with.** Nearly half of UK small-business owners say they have no one to discuss business issues with (American Express, March 2026, n=500) [secondary].
3. **Unguided AI use can backfire.** Unrestricted AI help raised practice scores but cut exam scores by 17%; structured use avoided the harm (Bastani et al., PNAS 2025) [V]. The programmes are designed around structured, measured use.
4. **Results are measured.** Every AI-Bench change records time, quality, rework and risk before and after, so partners receive evidence, not anecdotes.
5. **The organiser's track record:** PyPlym has changed careers, including one participant's journey from taxi driver to tech lead [F; with permission].

## 4. Partner roles

### Knowledge partner (Monument AI)

| Monument gives | Monument receives |
| --- | --- |
| Governance sessions within AI-Bench (for example vendor dependency, data, contracts and buyer-side questions), paid per session or by revenue share [A] | A trusted role with local leaders making real AI decisions |
| Advice on programme design | Visibility in Plymouth's practical AI community |
| Introductions to leaders who might join | Optional, disclosed referrals when a member wants independent governance advice |

**Principles:** AI-Bench is a peer group, not a sales room; nobody pitches; referrals in either direction are optional, disclosed and based on fit; participant details are never shared; EnReal owns the programme.

### How places are paid for: a full mix (decided 16 Oct)

No single funding route is locked in. Every place has **one published price**, whoever pays it.

| Route | Who | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Buy directly** | Individuals; small businesses | Pay the published price |
| **Organisation-funded** | An employer pays for its own leaders or staff | Pay the published price (invoice) |
| **Sponsored, for a group** | A sponsor funds places for a group it chooses, for example career changers, small businesses in Devonport, social enterprises | EnReal allocates places within that group against published criteria |
| **Sponsored, general** | A sponsor contributes to a general seat fund | EnReal allocates places to those who would otherwise miss out, against published criteria |

### One impact report, many uses ("do once, give many times")

Each cycle, EnReal produces **one standard impact report** with a common core and short views for each audience:

| Part | Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Core** (same for everyone) | Places filled, by route; who took part (anonymised: sector, size, career stage); workflows changed and measured (time, quality, rework, risk); tools built; what participants did next; case studies with permission |
| **Council view** | Local business growth and skills outcomes |
| **Employer view** (Babcock, Helsing and others) | Community and skills benefits, in a form they can use in their own social-value reporting |
| **College view** (City College Plymouth, to confirm) | Skills progression and routes into further learning |

**The same report is reused for:** the SoftOcean website showcase; PyPlym's season review; conversations with Monument AI and new sponsors; future funding applications; marketing to new participants.

### Seat sponsors (the Council; local employers; the college)

**A sponsored seat** pays for a place for someone who would benefit but could not easily pay.

| Sponsored seat | For | Why a sponsor might fund it | Indicative cost [A] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AI-Bench seat** | A leader of a small local business, charity or social enterprise | Local economic growth; supply-chain capability; community benefit | £1,800 per year (12 × £150) |
| **AI Workbench seat** | A **career changer** or someone new to technical work | Skills, inclusion, local talent; echoes the taxi-driver-to-tech-lead story | About £695 per cohort (organisation price) |

| Sponsor | Possible fit |
| --- | --- |
| **Plymouth City Council** | Local business growth and skills. Its funded business-support programmes (UK Shared Prosperity Fund strands such as SME support, start-up courses and social-enterprise support) ran until March 2026; a successor was not confirmed [V]. Ask what current routes exist rather than assume a budget. |
| **Babcock, Helsing and other local employers** | Community and skills commitments. Under the Procurement Act 2023 and the February 2025 National Procurement Policy Statement, public bodies must consider social value, meaning benefits for staff, suppliers or community **beyond** the contract's core deliverables; central government has applied a Social Value Model (PPN 002) since October 2025 [V]. **Scope matters:** PPN 002 covers specified central-government procurements and **excludes Ministry of Defence defence and security contracts**; a newer **PPN 026** (published 5 August 2026) applies a jobs-and-skills-focused model to relevant new procurements from **1 January 2027**, generally over £1m V, per the [16 October review (review 2026-10-16-vision, deleted; in git history)]. Neither shows that any named sponsor has a budget for this: ask which programme, budget owner, measures and decision date apply. Sponsored seats for local small businesses or career changers are the kind of community benefit suppliers report. Whether a specific sponsorship counts for a specific contract is the sponsor's judgement. |

**What every sponsor receives:** recognition as a sponsor; the impact report each cycle, with its own view; case studies with participants' permission. **What they do not receive:** participants' personal data, or influence over content.
